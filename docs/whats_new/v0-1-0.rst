v0.1.0 (Unreleased)
++++++++++++++++++++++

New Features
############
- The exergoeconomic analysis is no longer marked as beta: every component provides its auxiliary
  equations and its cost balance.
- The exergoeconomic analysis runs without split physical exergy as well. A material stream then
  carries a single cost rate for its physical exergy, ``C^PH``, instead of one for its thermal and
  one for its mechanical exergy, and the auxiliary equations of every component follow. Models that
  cannot provide the split, such as the ones exported from Aspen Plus, can be costed now. The split
  resolves the fuel and the product of a component more finely, so the specific costs of the two
  cases differ, and below the ambient temperature a component either keeps the cold exergy in its
  outlet stream or turns dissipative, with a warning that names it.
- The cost columns of the connection results follow the cost variables of the analysis: ``C^T`` and
  ``C^M`` with split physical exergy, ``C^PH`` without.
- A new example costs the CGAM process with and without split physical exergy and compares the two,
  component by component and stream by stream. See
  :ref:`Split Physical Exergy in Exergoeconomics <examples_split_physical_exergy_label>`.
- The CGAM example takes its investment cost rates from Kelly (2008), who evaluated the cost model
  of the CGAM problem for this base case, so the results can be held against the ones published
  there. The specific costs agree within about 6 % and the exergoeconomic factor within about 1.5
  points; the remaining difference comes from the thermodynamic model, real fluid properties here
  against the ideal gas with constant heat capacity of the reference.
- The solar thermal components take part in the exergoeconomic analysis. The heliostat field, the
  parabolic trough and the solar tower provide their auxiliary equations and their cost balance, so
  a solar plant can be costed end to end. The incoming radiation is a stream of the plant and
  carries a cost of its own, which is zero in the examples, and the optical and thermal losses of
  the collector field show up as exergy destruction rather than as a loss.
- The ``Drum`` provides its exergoeconomic balance.
- The ``Storage`` takes part in the exergoeconomic analysis. A storage that charges or discharges
  does not balance over its streams alone, so the analysis gives the exergy that stays behind in the
  store, or comes out of it, a cost variable of its own. That variable is priced like the physical
  exergy of the outlet, since the store is filled at the state of the outlet, and it enters the cost
  balance of the component and of the system on the side it flows to. A storage holding its level
  stores nothing and is costed like a pipe.
- The ``SteamGenerator`` takes part in the exergoeconomic analysis, with the drain priced like the
  high pressure steam, the mechanical and chemical exergy passed through, and both steam outlets
  produced at the same specific cost.
- A TESPy ``ParabolicTrough`` is a ``ParabolicTrough`` in ExerPy now, not a ``SimpleHeatExchanger``,
  and the parser adds the radiation on its aperture, :math:`E \cdot A`, as a heat connection named
  after the collector with a ``_Q`` suffix. The fuel of the collector is therefore the incoming
  radiation, as in the models exported from EBSILON.
- The solar thermal examples run an exergoeconomic analysis as well, and the parabolic trough plant
  exists as a TESPy model beside the EBSILON one. A test costs both and holds the results of every
  component of the one against the other, so the exergy of the streams, the auxiliary equations and
  the scaling of the solar field are checked across two simulators at once.
- :class:`~exerpy.analyses.EconomicAnalysis` turns the purchase equipment costs of the components
  into the cost rates the exergoeconomic analysis needs, with the total revenue requirement method of
  Bejan et al. (1996). It grows the equipment cost into the total capital investment, which the class
  did not do before and without which every cost rate was roughly six times too low, and levelizes
  the investment and the operating and maintenance cost over the lifetime of the plant.
- Every cost factor of the economic analysis may be given once for the whole plant or separately per
  component, so a heat exchanger can carry a different installation effort from a motor and a
  compressor a different maintenance share from a valve. Each component then carries its own
  investment and its own operating cost instead of the plant total being pooled and shared out by
  purchase equipment cost.
- Equipment quoted in an earlier year is carried to a reference year with a plant cost index, given
  per component or once for the whole plant, and given by the user. Where the reference year lies
  beyond the last entry of the index, the cost is carried with the index as far as it reaches and the
  remaining years are bridged with the nominal escalation rate, with a warning saying so. A year that
  the index does not cover at all is an error naming the year.
- The first-year operating and maintenance cost may be a share of the purchase equipment cost or of
  the total capital investment, selected with ``omc_basis``. The two differ by the installation
  factor, so a share meant for one basis is wrong on the other by that factor.
- The cost of the streams entering the plant is levelized over the lifetime of the plant as well,
  each with its own nominal escalation rate, so the cost of the fuel and the cost of the equipment
  are levelized over the same period and the specific cost of the product is a levelized cost too. A
  cost may be given per unit of exergy or per unit of energy. The levelized fuel cost and the
  complete total revenue requirement are part of the results.
- :meth:`~exerpy.analyses.EconomicAnalysis.compute_costs` returns the cost rates of the components
  and the costs of the entering streams in one dictionary, keyed for
  :meth:`~exerpy.analyses.ExergoeconomicAnalysis.run`, and checks both against the plant: a component
  without an equipment cost, a cost for something that is not in the plant, a cost for a component
  that carries no cost balance and a stream entering the plant without a cost are all reported.
  :meth:`~exerpy.analyses.EconomicAnalysis.compute_z` and
  :meth:`~exerpy.analyses.EconomicAnalysis.compute_c` return the two halves on their own.
  :meth:`~exerpy.analyses.EconomicAnalysis.economic_results` prints the levelized costs of the plant
  and the cost rate of every component.
- :meth:`~exerpy.analyses.EconomicAnalysis.from_json` reads the cost assumptions from a file, which
  keeps everything about one component in one entry instead of several dictionaries keyed by the same
  names. A value given for the whole plant applies to every component that does not name its own, so
  only what differs has to be written out, and a component or a stream cost may be a plain number
  instead of an object.
- A new example costs the heat pump from the equipment costs of its components, so the whole way from
  what a component costs to what its product costs is in one place. It comes with two cost files for
  the same plant, one with a single set of assumptions throughout and one with the quotes coming from
  three different years and the factors differing per component. See
  :ref:`From Equipment Costs to the Cost of the Product <examples_heatpump_exergoeconomic_label>`.
- The documentation has a page of its own on the economic analysis, with the nomenclature, the
  capital investment, the levelizing factors and the cost of the fuel, alongside the pages on the
  exergy and the exergoeconomic analysis.
- The exergoeconomic examples are part of the repository now. They cover the CGAM process, whose
  investment cost rates and fuel cost are the ones of the CGAM problem itself (Valero et al., 1994)
  instead of round numbers, and they run without any cost correlation module. The cost model is
  verified against the purchase costs the paper reports for its optimal design.

Bug Fixes
#########
- The relative cost difference ``r`` of the turbine, the compressor, the pump, the generator and the
  motor was computed from the cost rates, ``(C_P - C_F) / C_F``, instead of from the specific costs,
  ``(c_P - c_F) / c_F``. The two agree only where the exergy of fuel and product are equal, so the
  value was wrong wherever a component destroys exergy: for the compressor of the CGAM example it
  read 6.6 % instead of 14.7 %. The heat exchangers and the other components already used the
  correct definition.
- The ``c^TOT`` column of a heat or power stream in the table of the material connections was filled
  with the value of ``C^TOT``.
- A ``PowerBus`` with several inlets and a single outlet contributed no equation at all, which left
  the cost matrix singular. It writes its cost balance now.
- The ``PowerBus``, the ``Splitter`` and the ``Mixer`` assumed that their single inlet or outlet sits
  on connector 0. A parser is free to number the connectors as the model does, so the lookup raised a
  ``KeyError`` on models that do not start at 0.
- The cost of fuel and product of the ``FlashTank`` added up only one of the cost variables of a
  stream, so the two were not comparable quantities wherever the split or the chemical exergy added
  another one.
- The EBSILON parser attached the synthetic solar heat connection of a collector as an outlet of the
  component instead of an inlet, so the radiation left the collector rather than entering it.
- The console output of the analysis used characters outside of the encoding of a Windows console,
  which raised a ``UnicodeEncodeError`` instead of printing the report.
- The relative cost difference of the ``TOT`` row divided by a cost of fuel of zero, which happens
  whenever the fuel of the plant is free, as for a solar plant.
- The exergy fuel of the ``SteamGenerator`` was derived from the very streams it is meant to heat,
  so the component destroyed no exergy at all without split physical exergy and too little with it.
  It is the exergy of the incoming heat now.
- The cost of the product of a turbine was the cost of all the power leaving it, while its exergy
  product is the power it adds. On a shaft that carries the power of the stages before it, as the
  models exported from EBSILON have it, every stage but the first was therefore credited with the
  power of the stages in front of it: the specific cost of the product of the last stage of the
  parabolic trough example read 86.3 instead of 26.3 EUR/GJ.
- A collector standing in for ``num_branches`` identical parallel branches scaled the exergy balance
  but not the cost balance, so the plant was costed as if it had one branch. The cost balance of
  every component counts a branch stream as often as it exists now. Without it the parabolic trough
  example of EBSILON, whose solar field is 100 branches behind a header, lost 99 % of the cost of
  its field.
- The specific cost of an outlet of a ``Splitter`` that carries no exergy was equated with the cost
  of the inlet, which left a cost on a stream with nothing in it. Such an outlet costs nothing now.
- An investment cost given for a ``CycleCloser``, a ``PowerBus`` or a ``Splitter`` was swallowed:
  those components have no cost balance to charge it to. A non-zero cost for one of them is an error
  now, and a cost is no longer mandatory for a ``Splitter``.
- The ``Mixer`` divided by zero when its inlets were already at the state of its outlet.
- The auxiliary equations of the ``CycleCloser`` were written on the specific exergy of its streams
  instead of on their exergy flow, which is what the F-principle of the SPECO method asks for. The
  two agree for a cycle closer, whose inlet and outlet carry the same mass flow, so no result
  changes; every other component already used the exergy flow.
- The auxiliary equations of the ``FlashTank`` equated the cost per unit of specific exergy of its
  streams instead of their specific cost, so the two outlets were priced wrongly relative to each
  other whenever they did not carry the same mass flow. In a tank splitting a stream 3 to 7 the
  vapour came out 2.33 times as expensive as the liquid, instead of equally expensive.
- The exergy destruction ratio ``y*`` of a component divided by the exergy destruction of the plant
  without checking it against zero.
- The constant escalation levelization factor of the economic analysis was missing the factor ``k``
  that Bejan et al. put in front of it, so every levelized operating, maintenance and fuel cost came
  out too high, by ``1 + i_eff``, that is 12 % at a rate of return of 12 %. The factor now reproduces
  the worked example of the reference, and a test holds it against those numbers.
- The ``Storage`` assumed its streams sit on connector 0 and reported no fuel and no product at all
  when it neither charged nor discharged.
- The source distribution shipped the tests and the documentation of the CGAM exergoeconomic example
  but not the example itself, so neither the test suite nor the documentation could be built from it.

Documentation
#############
- The auxiliary equations of every component are written out as equations, for the analysis with and
  without split physical exergy. Where the two differ in more than the name of a cost variable, the
  docstring says so: a compressor, a pump, a mixer, a valve or a solar collector writes no rule at
  all on the physical exergy without the split, because the stream then carries a single cost
  variable that the cost balance determines, and the drum loses the rule coupling its thermal and
  its mechanical cost with the variables it coupled.
- The definitions that hold for every component, the specific costs of fuel and product, the cost of
  exergy destruction, the relative cost difference and the exergoeconomic factor, are stated once in
  :class:`~exerpy.components.component.Component` instead of being repeated in every component. Each
  component documents only its own cost of fuel and cost of product.
- The economic analysis states its equations as equations. The capital recovery factor, the cost
  escalation levelization factor, the levelized investment cost and the cost rate of a component
  were written as plain text, and two of them ran together into one unreadable line.
- The docstring of the auxiliary equations of the ``PowerBus`` described a cycle closer, a component
  it has nothing in common with.

API Changes
###########
- The cost that :class:`~exerpy.analyses.EconomicAnalysis` levelizes is the one at the beginning of
  the first year, in the price level of the reference year, as the reference defines it. An
  expenditure known for the first year of operation has to be divided by ``1 + r_n`` before it is
  given to the analysis.
- :class:`~exerpy.analyses.EconomicAnalysis` takes its parameters as arguments instead of one
  ``pars`` dictionary, and the purchase equipment costs as ``{"<component>": cost}`` instead of a
  list whose order had to match something unstated. ``PEC``, ``i_eff``, ``n``, ``tau``, ``f_tci`` and
  ``omc_share`` are required, since none of them can be defaulted without silently changing the
  result; only ``r_n`` and ``currency`` have defaults. ``compute_component_costs()`` takes no
  arguments any more and returns a dictionary keyed by component name, and
  ``compute_levelized_investment_cost()`` is gone: the investment now follows from the total capital
  investment of every component.
- The ``SteamGenerator`` takes the temperature of its heat source from a new ``T_hot`` attribute, in
  K. Without it the component falls back to the former approximation of its exergy fuel and warns.
- A TESPy model with a ``ParabolicTrough`` has to specify the irradiance ``E`` and the aperture area
  ``A`` of the collector, since the exergy fuel is the radiation on the aperture. Without them the
  parser falls back to the heat transferred to the fluid and warns that the optical and thermal
  losses are missing from the exergy destruction.
- ``aux_eqs()``, ``dis_eqs()`` and ``exergoeconomic_balance()`` of a component take an additional
  ``split_physical_exergy`` argument. Custom components have to accept it; it defaults to ``True``,
  so an implementation that ignores it keeps working as long as the split is used.

Testing
#######
- Every component is checked against the counting invariant of the cost matrix: it has to provide one
  auxiliary equation less than it has cost variables on its outlet streams (one more than that, and
  no cost balance, when it is dissipative). The check runs over three models, with and without split
  physical exergy and with and without chemical exergy.
- The parabolic trough plant is costed in both of its models, the one exported from EBSILON and the
  one built in TESPy, and the results of every component of the one are held against the other. That
  one test covers the exergy of the streams, the auxiliary equations of every component in the plant
  and the scaling of the solar field at once.
- The solar thermal components, the ``Storage`` and the ``SteamGenerator`` are covered, each with and
  without split physical exergy, over the cost balance of the component, the indicators it reports
  and the errors it raises when a stream it needs is missing.
- The four components that no costed example contains, the ``FlashTank``, the
  ``SimpleHeatExchanger``, the ``Storage`` and the ``SteamGenerator``, are costed on a plant of their
  own, so every component of the library takes part in an exergoeconomic analysis somewhere in the
  test suite.
