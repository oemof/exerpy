v0.2.0 (22.10.2026)
+++++++++++++++++++

New Features
############
- The purchased equipment cost of a component may be estimated with a cost correlation of OpenPyTEA
  :cite:`Tamarona2026`. The new module :mod:`exerpy.costing` lists the correlations that cost a
  class of component, suggests one per class, reads the size from the model, converts it into the
  units of the correlation and says how it relates to the range the correlation was fitted over. For a size
  outside the range, the cost is extrapolated from the nearest bound with a scaling exponent, or the
  component is split into parallel units. The costs are in US dollars of the year of the correlation and are converted with
  the annual exchange rates of the European Central Bank; the cost index of OpenPyTEA is read at
  runtime as well. ExerPy itself ships no cost correlation and no cost index. OpenPyTEA is an
  optional dependency, ``pip install exerpy[costs]``.
- :func:`~exerpy.costing.sizes` returns every size the model determines for a component, in SI
  units: the power, the mass flow, the volume flow at the inlet and the pressure ratio of a
  turbomachine, the power of a motor or a generator, and the heat flow, the heat transfer capacity, the
  logarithmic mean temperature difference and the area of a heat exchanger, and the heat flow and the
  live steam mass flow of a steam generator.
  :func:`~exerpy.costing.size` returns one of them. A user-defined cost equation takes the ones it needs,
  and :func:`~exerpy.costing.estimate` takes the one that has the dimension of the units of the
  chosen correlation, a power or a heat flow, an area, a volume flow or a mass flow, so the volume flow of
  a fan is converted into ACFM without a hand calculation.
- The area of a heat exchanger follows from its sections: the exchanger is cut at every phase
  change of either stream, and every section takes its own overall heat transfer coefficient
  :math:`U`. The coefficients are given per section, or taken from a new data file of published
  values: for each comparable cell (the exchanger type and construction and the fluid group and phase
  of each stream), the median of its values with their range, their number and their sources. A
  section without a comparable cell asks for its coefficient instead of borrowing one from another
  fluid, and a default that rests on fewer than three independent sources is reported. See
  :ref:`Component Sizing <component_sizing_label>`.
- The purchased equipment cost of a component may be scaled from the cost of a reference component of
  the same type and a different size, with the six-tenths rule or a user-defined exponent, and escalated from the
  year of the reference with the plant cost index. The size is given, or ``size_by`` names the size
  the reference refers to and that size is read from the model. See
  :ref:`Scaling the cost of a reference component <scaled_cost_label>`.
- :meth:`~exerpy.analyses.ExergyAnalysis.boundary_streams` lists the streams that cross the system
  boundary, with their direction, and :meth:`~exerpy.analyses.ExergyAnalysis.flows_from_roles` builds
  the fuel, product and loss definitions from the role of each of them, ``"F"``, ``"P"`` or ``"L"``.
- :meth:`~exerpy.analyses.EconomicAnalysis.from_dict` reads the cost assumptions from a dictionary
  with the structure of the file :meth:`~exerpy.analyses.EconomicAnalysis.from_json` reads.
- A reference year before the first or after the last entry of the plant cost index is bridged with the
  nominal escalation rate, forward or backward, with a warning saying so;
  :meth:`~exerpy.analyses.EconomicAnalysis.escalation_factor` returns the factor for a year.
- A ``SimpleHeatExchanger`` takes the temperature of the source of the heat it takes in as
  ``T_hot``, or of the consumer of the heat it gives off as ``T_cold``. The heat is then valued at that
  temperature, as the fuel or the product of the component, and the loss of the heat transfer counts
  as its exergy destruction. Without them, the heat is valued at the exergy change of the stream, as
  before. See :ref:`Heat from or to outside the model <heat_boundary_label>`.
- A new example obtains the purchased equipment costs of the heat pump in four ways (known costs,
  scaled costs of reference components, user-defined cost equations and the correlations of OpenPyTEA), carries
  each to the cost rates of the components and combines them. See
  :ref:`Purchased Equipment Costs from Four Sources <examples_purchase_costs_label>`.

Bug Fixes
#########
- The TESPy parser exports the vapour quality of a stream of one fluid, which the sections of a heat
  exchanger need to place a saturated end.
- The EBSILON parser exported the heat transfer capacity ``kA`` of a heat exchanger in the unit of
  the model, which may be kW/K; it now converts it into W/K.
- The specific volume of a stream was taken from the ``v`` of a TESPy export, which is its volume
  flow, so a mixture was placed as a gas or a liquid by its volume flow rather than by its specific
  volume.
- The sections of a heat exchanger are the same for TESPy, EBSILON and Aspen Plus models. The Aspen
  parser exports the volume flow and the vapour mass fraction of a stream; a stream whose export has
  no volume flow takes the phase of its fluid group; the gaseous water EBSILON lists a second time
  (``H2OG``) no longer hides a flue gas or air; and a stream that is saturated at both ends no longer
  needs the enthalpy reference of CoolProp.

Documentation
#############
- The documentation is regrouped into Get Started, Methods, Examples, Project and Reference, and the
  examples are sorted into exergy, economic and exergoeconomic analysis, each with an overview page.
  A table of the five steps of the workflow, from the exergy analysis to the exergoeconomic one,
  links the methods and the examples.
- A new page, :ref:`Component Sizing <component_sizing_label>`, explains which sizes a cost function
  takes, how the area of a heat exchanger is found and where the heat transfer coefficients come
  from, with a generated table of every value and its sources.
- The economic analysis page lists where the purchased equipment costs come from and names the symbol of
  every quantity it introduces.
- Every example ends with its complete code, read from the script itself, and every example of the
  economic and the exergoeconomic analysis shows the flowsheet of its plant.
- The example of the economic analysis of the heat pump stands on its own and carries a purchased
  equipment cost through every step to its cost rate. The example results are described as what they
  are, the outcome of placeholder costs, with a guide to reading them.
- The CGAM example no longer compares its results with those of Kelly (2008): her stream data do not
  follow the model of the CGAM problem, so the two are not comparable.
- OpenPyTEA and every source of the heat transfer coefficients are cited.
- The exergoeconomic analysis page explains how the equation system is built and solved, what
  changes with the split of the physical exergy and what each error message points to. The exergy
  analysis page explains how the heat exchanged with the outside of the model is valued, and the API
  overview covers all five steps of the workflow.

API Changes
###########
- The TESPy interface, ``exerpy[tespy]``, requires TESPy 0.11.3 or newer.
- OpenPyTEA 3.1.0 or newer is required for the cost estimates, through the new extra
  ``exerpy[costs]``.
- A purchased equipment cost given to :class:`~exerpy.analyses.EconomicAnalysis` may be a dictionary
  describing a scaled cost instead of a number.
- The years of :class:`~exerpy.analyses.EconomicAnalysis` are renamed: ``cost_year``, the year whose
  price level a cost from a source is given in, is now ``reference_year``, and the former
  ``reference_year``, the year every cost is carried to, is now ``base_year``. The attribute
  ``reference_PEC`` is now ``base_PEC``, and :func:`~exerpy.costing.estimate` returns the year of the
  correlation as ``reference_year``. The same keys apply in the files
  :meth:`~exerpy.analyses.EconomicAnalysis.from_json` reads.

Other Changes
#############
- A continuous integration check fails when a file contains a cost correlation or a table of
  a plant cost index, so that none enters the repository.
