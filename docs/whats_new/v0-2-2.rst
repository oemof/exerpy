v0.2.2 (10.11.2026)
+++++++++++++++++++

New Features
############
- The specific cost of a stream entering the plant can now be given for each exergy form, e.g.
  ``"<stream>_c_CH"``. A form without its own value takes ``"<stream>_c"``
  :cite:`lazzaretto2006speco` (closes #76).
- The cost of the loss streams is charged to the product streams in proportion to their net exergy
  (Bejan et al., Example 8.1) :cite:`Bejan1996`. A stream subtracted in ``E_P``, such as the feedwater
  of a boiler, is paired with the product stream it turns into downstream. Where that is not possible,
  the gross exergy is used as before and a warning names the stream.
- The cost of the exergy destruction of the whole plant, :math:`c_\mathrm{F,tot}\,\dot{E}_\mathrm{D,tot}`,
  is part of ``system_costs`` and of the JSON export.
- A cost correlation used outside its range gives a warning that names the correlation, the size and
  the bound the cost is extrapolated from.

Bug Fixes
#########
- The chemical exergy of a gas mixture from which water condenses at the ambient state was too
  high, since the exergy of the gas phase was not weighted with its molar fraction. The flue gases
  of the CCPP and CGAM examples had a chemical exergy that was 5 to 7 % too high. The exergy
  destruction of the combustion chamber was therefore too low and the exergy loss of the stack too
  high. The overall exergetic efficiency does not change (closes #63). The flue gas of the JSON
  example stored the old value, which is updated as well.
- In the Ahrendts library, the chemical exergy of liquid and gaseous methanol was swapped and
  deuterium had the value of oxygen. Deuterium has no value now.
- A pure substance gets the chemical exergy of its reference phase, e.g. liquid for methanol and
  ethanol. Before, it got the value of the gas, except for water.
- A pure substance without a value in the library gets a chemical exergy of 0 and a warning that
  says so. A mixture with such a substance raises a ``ValueError`` that names it. Before, a library
  entry without a value, such as krypton, raised a ``TypeError`` in a mixture. A stream without a
  composition, e.g. an EBSILON binary mixture, also gets 0 and a warning that names it.
- The docstrings of :func:`~exerpy.functions.calc_chemical_exergy` and
  :func:`~exerpy.functions.add_chemical_exergy` give the units of the ambient state in K and Pa and
  of the result in J/kg.
- The fuel cost of a mixer or a deaerator was wrong. With split physical exergy it did not contain the
  thermal exergy of its fuel streams. Without the split the exergy left in the outlet was valued at the
  cost of the outlet instead of the inlet, so the fuel cost depended on the investment cost of the
  component :cite:`lazzaretto2006speco`. Their c_F, c_P, C_D, r and f were wrong. Stream costs were not
  affected (closes #64).
- A dissipative heat exchanger or valve passed the full cost of an exergy form to an outlet without
  exergy of that form, e.g. the mechanical exergy of a stream leaving at the ambient pressure. This
  cost now stays in the cost difference of the dissipative component, which is charged to the other
  components. The valve treats the chemical exergy in the same way. In the heat pump example the costs
  of all components change, the cost of the product does not (closes #65).
- The share of the cost of dissipative components that is charged to a component is now shown in the
  new column Z_diss of the component results and in the JSON export, read directly from the cost
  matrix. It is part of the cost balance of the component and thus of its c_P and r
  :cite:`lazzaretto2006speco`. It is not part of f, which compares the investment of the component
  with the cost of its exergy destruction :cite:`Bejan1996`, nor of C_D+Z, since C_D+Z of the
  dissipative component already contains it. A stream that enters a dissipative component no longer
  relieves its source component of this cost in the cost matrix (closes #66).
- With split physical exergy, the exergy balance of the combustion chamber took the increase of
  physical exergy as product, its cost balance the increase of thermal exergy. Both now take the
  increase of thermal exergy as product and the decrease of chemical and mechanical exergy as fuel
  :cite:`lazzaretto2006speco`, summed over all inlets and outlets. The exergetic efficiency of the
  combustion chamber changes slightly, its exergy destruction does not (closes #67).
- An ambient state passed to ``from_aspen`` or ``from_ebsilon`` reached the components but not the
  exergies of the streams. Aspen now runs the model again at the passed state. EBSILON computes the
  physical exergy at it if it differs from the measuring points of the model. ``from_json`` raises a
  ``ValueError`` if the passed state differs from the one the exergies in the file were computed at
  (closes #68).
- The conversion of mass into molar fractions dropped species unknown to CoolProp without a warning.
  It now raises a ``ValueError`` that names them. The EBSILON parser maps its composition parameters
  and its pure fluids to CoolProp names, e.g. ETH to Ethane, and no longer writes parameters that are
  no mass fractions, such as the liquid share of water L_H2O (closes #69).
- When a stream had no exergy of one form, e.g. no mechanical exergy at the ambient pressure, the
  auxiliary cost equations of most components set a cost rate equal to a specific cost or fixed the
  cost of an inlet, which made the cost matrix singular. Such a stream now carries no cost of that
  form :cite:`lazzaretto2006speco`. A branch without mass flow no longer divides by zero (closes #71).
- In a closed cycle with chemical exergy the cost of the chemical exergy of the working fluid was
  undetermined, so the cost matrix was singular or ill-conditioned. The cycle closer now sets it to
  zero. If a stream enters the cycle from outside, e.g. make-up water, its cost carries through the
  cycle instead (closes #72).
- The auxiliary cost equations of the combustion chamber used only its first two inlets. They now use
  all inlets, an inlet without chemical exergy no longer makes the cost matrix singular, and a missing
  chemical exergy raises a clear error. An ash outlet is now taken into account: the ash is heated
  with the gas, so the increase of its physical exergy is product, while its chemical exergy is
  subtracted from the fuel. Its chemical exergy keeps the specific cost of the fuel, and the increase
  of its physical exergy costs as much per unit as that of the gas :cite:`lazzaretto2006speco`. The
  Aspen parser gives every stream of an RStoic block its own
  connector, so a second outlet no longer replaces the combustion gas, and it warns if an RStoic
  block has a heat duty but no heat stream (closes #73).
- A simple heat exchanger releasing heat above the ambient temperature had a negative product cost
  with split physical exergy and left out the investment cost without the split. Its product cost is
  now the cost of the heat. Below the ambient temperature its fuel multiplied two mass flows
  (closes #74).
- A collecting header with parallel branches counted every inlet, including its main inlet, as often as
  there are branches in the exergy balance, but only once in the cost balance. The mixer and the
  splitter now count a stream of a parabolic trough as often as the trough's ``num_branches`` and every
  other stream once (closes #75).
- With split physical exergy, the exergy balance of a mixer or a deaerator used the physical exergy,
  while its cost balance already used the thermal exergy and put the decrease of mechanical exergy into
  the fuel. Both now do the latter :cite:`lazzaretto2006speco`. Inlets of different composition put
  the decrease of their chemical exergy into the fuel as well, so the exergy destroyed by mixing is
  part of the exergy destruction. With the outlet exactly at the ambient temperature, the exergy that
  still leaves at the outlet is no longer counted as destruction, and the mixer no longer gives an
  exergy destruction of NaN there. An inlet now belongs to the fuel or the product by the sign of the
  change of its specific exergy, as SPECO requires :cite:`lazzaretto2006speco`, and no longer by its
  temperature, so steams of different pressures no longer give a negative product. The cost of the
  fuel follows the same streams and forms, also without the split and with the outlet at the ambient
  temperature. The vent of a deaerator and further outlets of a mixer are part of the balance and
  leave at the specific cost of the main outlet. In the examples, fuel and product of the deaerator
  and the mixer of the CCPP change by less than 0.01 kW.
- A simple heat exchanger taking in heat above the ambient temperature counted its investment cost in
  the cost of its fuel, so that r was close to zero. The cost of its fuel is now the cost of the heat.
- A simple heat exchanger without a heat stream wrote one auxiliary cost equation too many when it
  released heat, which made the cost matrix fail. It now leaves the outlet to its cost balance, and it
  warns that the heat then has no cost.
- A simple heat exchanger cooling a stream across the ambient temperature without split physical
  exergy could have a negative exergy destruction. Without the split it now uses the physical exergy
  only, as chosen: the heat carries the net change of physical exergy, and a warning explains that the
  warm and the cold exergy cannot be told apart. Without the split, a simple heat exchanger with a
  pressure drop warns that the exergy lost by the pressure drop is counted in the exergy of the heat,
  not as exergy destruction.
- A dissipative heat exchanger gave the auxiliary fluid, such as the cooling water, the specific cost
  it entered with, so it left with more cost than it brought. The auxiliary fluid now leaves without
  cost, so its cost is part of the cost of the dissipative component, while the main working fluid
  keeps its specific cost :cite:`lazzaretto2006speco`. The auxiliary fluid is found from the plant: it
  is the side whose outlet reaches a loss stream of ``E_L`` through splitters, mixers and dissipative
  components only, on every branch. Where that does not decide, a stream that gains exergy is taken as
  the auxiliary fluid. A heat exchanger whose product reaches ``E_L`` this way, e.g. cooling water that
  joins other waste water in a mixer before it is discharged, is now dissipative as well, and not
  only when the product outlet itself is listed in ``E_L`` :cite:`Bejan1996`. The cost of the fuel of a dissipative heat exchanger contains every cost its streams leave
  behind, the chemical cost of the auxiliary fluid included, so that its C_D + Z is its cost
  difference. In the heat pump example the air leaving the evaporator now has no cost. The cost of the
  product does not change, the costs of the components change by less than 0.5 %.
- A simple heat exchanger heating a stream from below to above the ambient temperature with split
  physical exergy had a negative exergy destruction. Its product is now the thermal exergy of the
  outlet, and the cold exergy of the inlet is destroyed, as when a stream is cooled across the ambient
  temperature :cite:`Bejan1996`.
- A turbine whose outlet leaves slightly warmer than its inlet, such as a hydraulic turbine, and a
  compressor or pump with inlet and outlet exactly at the ambient temperature had no fuel and product.
  They now keep their definition. The cost part of the turbine uses the same outlet as its exergy part,
  and turbomachines no longer fail when their fuel or product cannot be defined.
- The steam generator looked for its heat only among its inlets and took the drain from outlet 2,
  while the EBSILON parser maps the heat to connector 2 and the drain to outlet 3. It now finds the heat
  by its kind and the drain as the material outlet besides the two steam outlets, and a missing drain
  is allowed.
- The exergy balance of a flash tank used only the thermal exergy with split physical exergy, so its
  exergy destruction could be negative, while its cost balance used all exergy forms. Both now take
  the thermal exergy of the inlet as fuel and that of the outlets as product, and the decrease of
  mechanical and chemical exergy as fuel. A storage now counts the chemical exergy in its exergy
  balance, as it did in its cost balance.
- A valve below the ambient temperature whose liquid warms on throttling had a negative product. It
  is now dissipative :cite:`lazzaretto2006speco`.
- A heat exchanger with a stream at a third connector, such as a cascaded drain mapped from EBSILON,
  left that stream out of its balance. It now raises an error that names the connector.
- With split physical exergy, the parabolic trough wrote its fuel, which includes the decrease of
  mechanical exergy of the fluid, onto the solar heat connection. The connection now carries the
  solar exergy only.
- The relative cost difference r of a component without a product was shown as infinite. It is now
  NaN. The exergoeconomic factor of a generator or motor without exergy destruction and investment
  was 0 instead of NaN, several components divided by zero for r and f, and the motor swapped fuel and
  product in its exergy balance but not in its costs. These are corrected.
- A turbine expanding to or below the ambient temperature with split physical exergy wrote no
  auxiliary cost equations, so the cost matrix was singular. The mechanical exergy now keeps its
  specific cost, and the thermal exergy of the outlet costs as much per unit as the power
  :cite:`lazzaretto2006speco`.
- ``flows_from_roles`` counted the exergy of a heat stream in the direction of the heat. Below the
  ambient temperature the exergy flows against the heat :cite:`Bejan1996`, so such a heat stream is now
  reversed, with a warning.
- The cost of the exergy loss charged to the products left out loss streams listed as ``outputs`` of
  ``E_L``, so a priced one, such as purchased cooling water, broke the cost balance of the plant. The
  loss cost is now the net value :cite:`lazzaretto2006speco`. ``check_cost_balance()`` called after
  ``run()`` no longer shows the loss share of a power or heat product as a residual.
- A stream listed in ``E_F``, ``E_P`` or ``E_L`` without an exergy value made the totals NaN. It now
  raises an error that names it. A heat exchanger in a temperature configuration without fuel and
  product and a parabolic trough below the ambient temperature now give their exergy destruction from
  the balance instead of NaN.
- A simple heat exchanger taking in heat below the ambient temperature with split physical exergy
  counted the pressure drop against its product. The pressure drop is now fuel and destroyed, as for a
  heat exchanger below the ambient temperature :cite:`Bejan1996`.
- The cost of a heat stream followed the direction of the heat. Below the ambient temperature the
  exergy of heat flows against the heat :cite:`Bejan1996`, and its cost now does too, with a warning.
  Heat taken in by a stream below the ambient temperature, as in an evaporator that cools a room, is
  the product of the simple heat exchanger, which prices it, so it needs no cost. Heat given off by a
  stream below the ambient temperature or by a stream cooled across it, as in a chiller, brings the
  cold exergy and needs a cost like any fuel entering the plant. These cases used to give a negative
  fuel cost or a singular cost matrix. Without split physical exergy, a stream heated across the
  ambient temperature now uses the net change of physical exergy as well.
- The Aspen parser wrote the work stream of a compressor as leaving it, and the electric power of the
  motor that drives it as leaving the motor. A pump got a second electric inlet next to its own work
  stream. Power now flows from the grid through the motor into the compressor or pump, and the work
  stream of a pump is the electric inlet of its motor. The exergoeconomic analysis of the Aspen CCPP
  model solves.
- A second call of ``analyse()`` kept the dissipative behaviour inferred from the first one and the
  rows of the earlier cost matrix. Compressor and pump now give the exergy destruction from the exergy
  balance when the outlet is colder than the inlet. A heat exchanger warns if its hot side enters
  colder than its cold side. The check of the overall exergy balance no longer skips a component
  whose exergy destruction is NaN.

Other Changes
#############
- The examples were run again with all three simulators. Their stored results, the validation
  notebooks and the results of the exergoeconomic examples in the documentation are up to date. The
  documentation of the heat pump exergoeconomic example now shows the results with split physical
  exergy, as its script computes them. The deviations between the simulators do not change.
- The table of the number of cost equations of the CGAM example in the documentation was out of date
  and is corrected.
- ``EconomicAnalysis.first_year_om_costs()`` is renamed to ``base_year_om_costs()``, since it returns
  the cost at the start of the first year in money of the base year. The old name still works with a
  ``DeprecationWarning``.
- The docstrings of the pump and of the heat exchanger without split physical exergy now match the
  code.
